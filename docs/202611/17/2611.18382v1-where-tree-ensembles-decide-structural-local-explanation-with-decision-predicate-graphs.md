## 摘要
树集成是表格数据上最准确的模型之一，但单次预测背后的计算分散在数百条决策规则中。特征归因和反事实通过特征影响或输入变化来解释这类预测，却未说明预测响应的是树所应用的哪些阈值；而结构化解释会压缩这些规则，并丢失其背后的树。为弥补这一空白，我们提出局部决策谓词图（local DPG），它将样本所执行的规则汇集为可验证的计算记录，并带有可复现每个被解释预测的类别支持度。从这些规则中，我们选择关键谓词，即估计其跨越会使模型最强烈地移向所选类别的已执行谓词；它为用户提供一个具体阈值以及共享该阈值的树，且无需外部解释器。在16个数据集和四个集成模型族上，它区分不同预测邻居的能力优于随机谓词和最近阈值谓词。单次跨越可在46–69%的错误预测中恢复真实类别；而将同一规则应用于TreeSHAP排名第一的特征时，这一比例为29–50%，该指标接近选择准则；在植入故障上，它能在46–71%的由此产生的错误中找到故障阈值，而随机方法为6–21%。尽管对这些谓词进行免训练编辑并未降低未见数据上的误差，关键谓词仍通过指明预测可在何处被改变，补充了归因和反事实解释。

## Abstract
Tree ensembles are among the most accurate models for tabular data, yet the computation behind a single prediction is spread over hundreds of decision rules. Feature attributions and counterfactuals explain such predictions through the influence of features or a change of the input, leaving open which of the thresholds applied by the trees a prediction responds to, while structural explanations compress these rules and lose the trees behind them. To address this gap, we propose a local Decision Predicate Graph (local DPG), which gathers the rules executed for a sample into a verifiable record of the computation, with a class support that reproduces every explained prediction. From these rules we select the critical predicate, the executed predicate whose crossing is estimated to move the model most strongly toward a chosen class, which offers the user a concrete threshold and the trees that share it, without an external explainer. Across 16 datasets and four ensemble families, it separates differently predicted neighbours better than random and nearest-threshold predicates. A single crossing recovers the true class in 46-69% of wrong predictions, against 29-50% when the same rule is applied to the feature ranked first by TreeSHAP, a measure close to the selection criterion, and on planted faults it finds the faulty threshold in 46-71% of the resulting errors, against 6-21% at random. Although training-free edits of these predicates did not reduce the error on unseen data, the critical predicate complements attributions and counterfactuals by naming where a prediction can be changed.
